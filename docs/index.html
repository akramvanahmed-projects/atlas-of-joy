Four ways to guess

Four models try to rank 67 countries by happiness. Each country is guessed without ever seeing its own score. The numbers show how well each guess matches each report, as a rank correlation. 1 would be perfect, 0 is a coin toss.

      Two countries

      One everybody expects near the top, and one that surprises people. Ranks are out of 67 countries, 1 is happiest.

      The one you'd expect

      Norway

      Rich, safe and always near the top of the happiness tables. The photos are mostly landscapes: half of them show nature, and fewer than one in ten show people together. The model ranks Norway about where the surveys do, but that's mostly the money talking.

      The one you wouldn't

      Mexico

      A quarter of Norway's GDP per head, yet it sits in the top 15 of every survey here. Mexicans photograph each other a lot: 15% of their photos show people together, against a typical 9%. That's the pattern the atlas found. Even so, the model still puts Mexico far too low.

      How to read this

      The photos were taken between 2004 and 2014 by people photographing their own country. The happiness scores come from the same years.

      This is a correlation across 69 countries, not cause and effect. Photographing friends doesn't make a country happier. More likely, places where social life is visible and valued score higher on both.

      Flickr users aren't a random slice of any country. They are probably younger, better off and more urban than average, and the gap is likely bigger in poorer countries.

      We tested 18 signals. Some of them will look good by luck. The people signals survive a placebo test, but treat them as a lead, not a law.
